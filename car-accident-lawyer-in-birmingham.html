Birmingham has long drawn travelers for its role in American civil rights history, but the city's appeal has expanded well beyond a single chapter of the national story.History and Heritage at the Center of Birmingham's IdentityThe updated guide places Birmingham's civil rights history at the forefront, highlighting the Birmingham Civil Rights Institute and the surrounding Civil Rights Heritage Trail as essential stops for any visitor. The institute offers extensive documentation of the American civil rights movement, situated in the Fourth Avenue Historic District alongside 16th Street Baptist Church. Together, these sites form one of the most historically significant corridors in the American South.The guide also spotlights Rickwood Field, recognized as the oldest professional baseball park still in use in the United States. The stadium has served as a backdrop for major motion pictures and continues to host special events that draw baseball enthusiasts from across the country. Its preservation represents a distinct piece of American sporting history that few cities can claim.Outdoor Spaces and Cultural Landmarks Worth the TripAmong the things to do in Birmingham, Alabama, Vulcan Park and Museum ranks as one of the most recognizable experiences in the city. The park is home to the largest cast-iron statue in the world, the Roman god Vulcan, which overlooks the city from atop Red Mountain. The attached museum provides context on Birmingham's industrial past, and the observation deck delivers sweeping views of the surrounding landscape.Railroad Park, a 19-acre green space in the heart of downtown, has become a gathering point for residents and visitors alike. The park connects to the wider trail network and sits within walking distance of several neighborhoods, making it a practical and scenic stop for those exploring the city on foot.Food, Motorsports, and the Alabama Theatre Round Out the ExperienceThe guide gives notable attention to Birmingham's growing restaurant and food scene, which has drawn attention from food publications in recent years. The adjacent road course hosts professional racing events throughout the year, drawing motorsports enthusiasts who might not otherwise put Birmingham on their travel list.The historic Alabama Theatre, built in 1927, continues to host film screenings, concerts, and live events. The venue's Moorish Revival interior and its original Wurlitzer organ have made it a landmark for those interested in vintage architecture and performance spaces."Birmingham often surprises first-time visitors because there is so much more to the city than they expect," said Anas Hameed, Head of Marketing at Passports With Purpose. "Whether someone is drawn to civil rights history, live music, outdoor spaces, or food, there is something genuinely worth the trip.
